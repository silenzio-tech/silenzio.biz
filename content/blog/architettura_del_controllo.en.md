Observing the direction our society has taken often offers the sensation of being inside a major, deeply flawed construction project, where the foundations progressively give way under the weight of increasingly pervasive surveillance.

In this scenario, it is worth asking ourselves how the perimeter of **cybersecurity** has changed. There was a time when computer security responded to limited needs: keeping bad guys away from bank accounts or securing servers against digital extortion. Today, the issue seems to have expanded out of all proportion, transforming into something much more complex and, in some ways, disheartening.

The contemporary challenge no longer concerns only protection from external or criminal actors, but raises an uncomfortable question: to what extent have the digital tools we use every day become vehicles of overwhelming state and institutional control?

### Technology as a space for negotiation

The tech industry and the structured systems we rely on no longer appear as neutral spaces of progress. They often tend to require a silent surrender of autonomy in exchange for efficiency and convenience. This dynamic opens up a necessary reflection: if digital infrastructures can potentially turn against individual freedoms, doesn't the protection of one's data risk becoming a form of daily resistance?

It is not a question of embracing catastrophic visions or indicating dogmatic solutions, but of acknowledging that the use of **OPSEC (Operations Security)** practices, decentralized networks and encryption tools no longer represents just a niche technical skill. For many, today, it has become a space of awareness and civil self-defense.

### An open space for discussion

Perhaps the real question we should ask ourselves is not how to passively adapt to a digital ecosystem that reduces our margins of privacy, but which spaces of personal sovereignty we are willing to preside over.

The debate remains open. Faced with the drift of a future that slides towards hyper-control, maintaining clarity and continuing to question ourselves is already a first step.
