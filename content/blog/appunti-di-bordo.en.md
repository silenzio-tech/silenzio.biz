There is a precise moment when you get used to the dark. When you live long protected by the anonymity of hidden circuits, opaque protocols and encrypted strings, darkness becomes your normality. You learn to move without making noise, to calibrate every step, to consider the surface of the network as a hostile territory, a monitored transit place from which to stay away.

Yet, at a certain point, the question changes. It's no longer *"how safe are we down here?"*, but *"what's the point of writing, building and communicating if the message remains confined to a niche of shadows?"*

Today I took the next step.

I haven't shut down, I haven't turned off the old channels: the site is now *also* free-to-air. It is a double track, a calculated coexistence. On the one hand, the protection and isolation of closed circuits; on the other, the surface, the *clearnet*, which opens to anyone who happens to pass by, without the need for special keys, dedicated configurations or intermediate steps.

Moving content out there too feels strange. It's almost dizzying: you know that the traffic is different, that the background noise is deafening and that the surface is dominated by logics that often don't belong to you. Yet, the real challenge is precisely this: to inhabit the light of the open network without being swallowed up, maintaining the same rigor, the same care and the same autonomy that you put into things that remain hidden.

An extension of the perimeter, an experiment to see the effect that makes the two souls of digital space coexist.

Let's see how he reacts.
