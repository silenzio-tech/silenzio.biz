Talking about digital self-defense often means colliding with two extremes: on the one hand total disinterest, on the other the dogmatic belief that simply installing the application of the moment is enough to protect yourself from any threat. In this panorama, **[Surveillance Self-Defense](https://ssd.eff.org/)**, the historic guide of the Electronic Frontier Foundation, deserves to be read not as a handbook to be followed blindly, but as an excellent personal training exercise.



However, approaching this kind of resource requires a critical look. The risk to avoid is falling into the "toolbox" illusion: that is, thinking that security can be solved by downloading recommended software or relying on standardized solutions. Protecting your data is not a pre-packaged package, but a process of continuous awareness. Furthermore, much of this manual tends to move within dominant technological ecosystems, inevitably offering partial answers to those seeking radical autonomy or the construction of independent infrastructures.

Considering *Surveillance Self-Defense* as an end point would be a mistake; it should instead be seen as a fundamental text for one's basic training. No educational portal will ever be able to replace study, reduction of the attack surface and direct control of one's hardware. Reading it helps you understand the rules of the current playing field, and then clearly decide how to reshuffle them.
